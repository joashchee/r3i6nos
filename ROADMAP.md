# Roadmap

What is planned and what is left (rechecked against the unit's reports on 2026-11-11, and against the sister repositories and this repo's own work on 2026-11-18: section 32). Each section names the
document that has the detail. **Apps have run on a real R36S since 2026-11-09** (KlamANSI, Neumetik, Composer, Painter, Animator, NoCoGaMa, TinyNTT, the IO Checker, r3i6nos's own report); a claim is still "untested" until a report or the maintainer says otherwise.

**Effort ratings.** Every open item starts with its effort, `- [ ] [S]`: **S** an hour or less (one small change, one doc, one session on the unit and reading the report), **M** a few hours to a day (a feature or fix with its tests), **L** several days (a subsystem part, several files or apps, or several rounds on the unit), **XL** a week or more (a new app or subsystem, a change across every app, or something that waits on outside work). It rates the hands-on work left once nothing blocks it; what blocks an item is said in the item. Pick by effort with `scripts/roadmap_effort.py --list S` (or `M`, `L`, `XL`; `--count` totals them); `scripts/check.sh` fails on an open item with no rating. Done items need none.

## 7. Phase 6: slim and release

- [ ] [XL] **A mainline kernel instead of the vendor 4.4.189: estimated 2026-11-11, not decided** (`docs/MAINLINE-KERNEL.md`). ROCKNIX (officially, "Mainline Linux", libmali or Panfrost) and Arch R (6.12 LTS, Mesa Panfrost) already run the genuine R36S on one. **About 40-75 working days in all (8 to 15 weeks of focused work; calendar time is longer because panel variants need testers); a first slice that boots our image to the menu on the one unit, with graphics, sound, buttons and a battery reading, is about 20-30 days.** It would also shrink rule 2 to the bootloader and the card's panel data, make `r3i6nos-setup` take seconds, and unblock Debian 13 and later. Biggest risks: panel and board revisions (we own one unit), Panfrost against the Mali blob, and re-measuring audio, input, battery and hot-plug. **Order: trials T1-T5 first, then the half-day spike (boot ROCKNIX's RK3326 image on the unit and read its kernel config, DTS and patches), then decide.**
- [ ] [M] **Debian 13 (trixie) instead of 12 (bookworm): evaluated 2026-11-11, decision: stay on 12 for now.** Measured with a native arm64 `debootstrap` of the same package list: **663 MiB vs 664 MiB** unpacked (228 vs 240 packages), so size is no reason either way. Trixie has systemd 257.13, glibc 2.41 (its minimum-kernel tag is 3.7.0: fine with 4.4), SDL 2.32.4 (bookworm 2.26.5), Python 3.13.5 (nothing we import was removed: scanned), LuaJIT 2.1.0+openresty20250117 (bookworm 2.1.0~beta3), OpenSSH 10.0. **The one real risk is systemd on the 4.4.189 kernel:** upstream says kernels below 5.4 get "limited testing" and an `old-kernel` taint (v257), cgroup v1 is "obsolete" and **ignored unless `SYSTEMD_CGROUP_ENABLE_LEGACY_FORCE=1` is on the kernel command line** (v256/v257 NEWS), and v258 makes 5.4 the minimum and drops cgroup v1; the stock kernel does contain a `cgroup2` filesystem (seen in the captured `Image`), so v2 may mount, but nobody has booted it. ## 38. Hardening the saved settings on the data card (maintainer's question 2026-11-19, recommendations accepted, to be built later; threat model and reasoning in `docs/SAVED-SETTINGS.md`, "Hardening, planned")
Why: what `r3i6nos-persist` puts on the data card for Wi-Fi is the network's **WPA key** (the 256-bit PBKDF2 result; the password itself is never stored). It cannot be turned back into the password, but **it lets anyone join that network**, and on
an exFAT or FAT card (no file permissions) it is readable by anyone who holds the card **and by any game or port the unit runs** (they run as `loveos` and read `/roms2`). Worse, the rule "a card this unit has not met wins" lets a card from elsewhere
**plant a network** that the unit may later join, or a clock far in the future. A PIN alone does not fix this: whoever holds the card can try every PIN offline (a 4-digit PIN is 10,000 guesses, a 6-digit one a million; even scrypt at
a fraction of a second a guess on a PC is minutes to about a day for 6 digits), and the unit cannot afford a much slower unlock. **The recommendation (accepted 2026-11-19): a unit-bound key, no PIN needed; a PIN or passphrase only as an optional extra.**
**Nothing here is built.**
- [ ] [S] **0. Check the unit first** (a device-report line, then **blocked on the maintainer's unit**): is there a per-unit value that sits on neither card and that only root can read, and is it stable across reboots and a reflash? Candidates: the SoC's one-time-programmable block (`otp@ff290000`, `rockchip,px30-otp` is in the stock device tree and `nvmem-rockchip-otp.ko` ships; its cpu-id cell is not named in this device tree, so the raw nvmem and `/proc/cpuinfo`'s `Serial` are to be tried), and the vendor storage. Report: which source, its length, its file mode (it must not be readable by `loveos`), and whether two boots agree. **Everything below that depends on the unit-bound key waits for this.**
- [ ] [L] **1. A unit-bound key**: `HKDF-SHA256(unit id, a random salt kept on the card, "r3i6nos-persist v1 <item>")`, one key for each item and purpose (encrypt, authenticate). The key is never written anywhere. Survives a reflash of the same unit; a card from another unit cannot be read. Standard library for HKDF, HMAC and scrypt; `openssl` (already in the image as a dependency of `ca-certificates`) for AES-256-CTR, as encrypt-then-MAC with HMAC-SHA256 (Python has no AES; no new package).
- [ ] [L] **2. Authenticate every item on the card, encrypt the secret ones.** A versioned header (magic, version, salt, nonce, MAC) so the format can change. **Every item is authenticated** (so a card from elsewhere can no longer plant a network, a zone or a time: an item whose MAC fails is not restored, is kept in `.merge-conflicts/` and is reported as "from another unit"); **the Wi-Fi key is encrypted** (the rest need not be: a person can still read a zone; the maintainer to confirm, or encrypt all for uniformity). This **replaces** the rule "a card the unit has not met wins": only an authenticated copy can win. Constant-time MAC comparison.
- [ ] [M] **3. Migration**: an existing plaintext `wifi.json` on a card is rewritten encrypted at the next sync and the plaintext removed; the unit's own copy is unchanged (root-only on the rootfs).
